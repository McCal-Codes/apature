Apature brand · v0.1 · 2026-10-14

Fast hands, friendly face.

Apature is a photojournalist's tool: cull, caption, ingest, deliver, on deadline. The interface is dark, dense and quiet. The brand is the one place it smiles, and the smile has a name: Snap.

One character does all the work. Head-on and still, Snap is the logo mark and the app icon. With arms and a pose, Snap is the mascot for empty states, finished jobs and the README.

Guidelines

Voice & tone

Apature talks like a good photo editor on deadline: direct, specific, calm. It says what happened and what to do next. It never apologises in a dialog, never jokes while a card is copying, and never hides a failure behind a friendly word.

Say what happened, with numbers."Every file verified at every destination · 242 MB in 2.2 s."
Name the next action."2 files failed. The card has not been changed; fix the destination and ingest again."
Let Snap carry the warmth.The copy stays plain; the character supplies the smile.
No "Oops!", "Yay!" or exclamation marks in the app.Snap can look surprised; the text cannot.
No jargon the photographer did not choose."Sidecar", "IIM", "colour class" are theirs; "fingerprint ledger" is ours and stays in the docs.
No motion or mascot on the contact sheet itself.Culling is the work; nothing competes with the pictures.
Guidelines

Visual elements

One design system, two appearances. Apature Dark is a professional darkroom: deep charcoal, compact controls, lavender highlights. Apature Light is a Mac studio: off-white, system blue, open spacing. Snap wears the dark body in both, so the character stays the same on either ground.

Spacing

A 4 pt scale everywhere: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32. Snap's clear space is one lens diameter on every side; the mark is never smaller than 16 pt, the full-body mascot never smaller than 48 pt.

Library

Logo library

The mark is Snap head-on with no limbs: a camera silhouette with two eyes and a lens. Use the dark-body mark on both grounds; the mono versions are for one-colour print, engraving and the menu bar. The app icon puts the off-white body on the charcoal squircle so it reads on the Dock.

Wordmark

"Apature" set in SF Pro Display Semibold, tracking −2 %. The SVGs reference the system font; outline the text before sending the wordmark anywhere SF is not installed.

Library

Mascot library

Snap is a small camera with a lens for a belly, two eyes, blush, and lavender arms and legs. The body never changes colour. Arms, eyes and mouth carry every expression. Eight poses cover the moments in Product design; new poses reuse these parts.

Expressions

Keep the lens.The lens is the belly and the identity. It never becomes an eye, a mouth or a button.
Face front.Snap turns its eyes, not its body. Three-quarter views are a later addition, drawn once and added here.
No props beyond the card.No hats, phones, coffee. The SD card is the only object Snap holds.
No recolouring.Lavender limbs on a charcoal body on any ground; mono only in the logo library.
Library

Pictogram library

Line pictograms on a 24 px grid, 2 px round stroke, one colour (lavender on dark, lavender deep on light). They match the painter-drawn toolbar icons in the app and are for docs, the README and marketing, not for the toolbar itself.

Backdrops for the README, release notes and the About window. The contact-sheet grid sits at 8 % so a photo or Snap can go over it.

Library

Motion library

One curve, two durations, nothing under Reduce Motion. The app's rules (ease-out, 120 ms micro, 160 ms layout, 600 ms done flash) apply to Snap too.

BlinkBoth eyes close for 120 ms every 4 to 6 s, randomised. Never while a progress bar moves.
WaveHello only: the arm swings twice over 900 ms on first launch of a session, then rests.
CheerArms rise over 160 ms when a job finishes clean, hold 600 ms with the card's accent flash, then rest.
ThinkEyes drift right, then left, 2 s loop, while a decode is pending more than 2 s.
SleepA "z" fades in every 3 s on the empty sheet; the body breathes 2 px over 4 s.
Reduce MotionAll of the above become cuts; Snap simply shows the pose.
