Mascot research for Apature

Second, extended pass, 2026-10-14. Six research threads (cuteness science, software mascot case studies, animal vision facts, name availability, in-app motion rules, newsroom culture) ran in parallel; this document is the synthesis. Claims are tagged measured (a study), reported (brand teams, press, primary pages) or inferred (our reading). Anything a thread could not confirm is marked UNVERIFIED. Sources are at the end of each section.

0. The short version

  1. Cuteness is mostly geometry, and it does not wear off. Big head, eyes set low and large (about 40 % of head height is the ceiling that Mickey Mouse arrived at over fifty years), round body, stubby limbs, very few features. The response is pre-attentive and survived repeat exposure in the one product study that tested it. (§1)
  2. Software mascots that last live on the splash, the empty state and the small win, and never on the working surface. Clippy and the "evil Duo" meme were both interruptions of the task; Kit and Kiki step aside. Photo tools have no mascots at all, which is an open lane and a warning. (§2, §4)
  3. A looping idle animation next to other content is a WCAG Level A problem, not a nicety. Mascot motion must be one-shot, under five seconds, on user events only, and off entirely under Reduce Motion. Ship a "no mascot" switch. (§4)
  4. Licence the character CC BY 4.0 (or CC0) with a no-trademark clause, and keep the name and app icon as the project's marks. This is the Go gopher / Android / Kiki model. (§3)
  5. "Lumen" is taken three times over inside photography (Lumen Camera, Lumen Forge, Rollei Lumen). Rename the character. (§5)
  6. The owl is defensible on the science and crowded on the shelf (Duolingo, Hootsuite, Tripadvisor). Two animals beat it on story and ownership: the jumping spider, which has the most camera-like eye in nature, and the homing pigeon, which newspapers really used to fly film from the ballpark to the darkroom. The kingfisher collides with a major Swift image library; the firefly with Adobe Firefly; the eagle with an existing photo organiser and with the owner's own paper. (§6)
  7. Two verified facts are brand gold: lavender oil was the solvent in Niépce's first photographs (the accent colour is photography's origin), and the firefly is Pennsylvania's state insect. (§7)

1. Why things read as cute (science)

Lorenz's releasers, as Gould quotes them (reported): "a relatively large head, predominance of the brain capsule, large and low-lying eyes, bulging cheek region, short and thick extremities, a springy elastic consistency, and clumsy movements."

Gould's Mickey Mouse measurements (measured, from the essay): over three design stages, eye height went from 27 to 42 % of head length, head length from 42.7 to 48.1 % of body length, and nose-to-front-ear from 71.7 to 95.6 % of nose-to-rear-ear. The rival Mortimer had a head 29 % of body length and a snout 80 % of head length. Because Mickey's head is a fixed circle, juvenility came from moving the ears back, not from reshaping the head.

Glocker et al. 2009 (measured): manipulating infant photos towards the schema raised cuteness ratings and caretaking motivation. Frontiers 2022 (measured): low placement of features and a narrow jaw predicted cuteness; forehead height did not.

Miesler, Leder & Herrmann 2011, car fronts (measured): headlights +20 %, grille −20 %, intake narrower and taller; babyfaced cars rated cuter (d = 0.54), with smile-muscle activation, no habituation across exposures, and no change in attractiveness. Their advice: exaggerate the key stimulus, "such as very large headlights."

Eyes (measured, mixed): enlarging eyes 15 % helped only by avoiding small eyes; beyond that looked unnatural in realistic faces (Yao 2022). A 2024 Cognition study found larger irises with smaller pupils most attractive, and a 2025 primate-portrait study found bright irises cuter and friendlier only with dilated pupils: a bright ring with a medium-large dark centre is the safe reading. One consistent highlight at the same clock position on both eyes reads as alive and looking at you (Kitaoka, Anstis); mismatched highlights read cross-eyed.

Craft rules that match the science (reported): Bruna's Miffy is dots for eyes and a cross for a mouth, front-facing, hundreds of sketches per expression; Kirby is "a simple circular design, so anyone could draw him"; Pikachu was slimmed not for cuteness but because the anime needed "human-like gestures"; Kumamon is a black body, red cheeks and a "constant look of surprise"; Hello Kitty's missing mouth lets people "project their feelings onto the character" (Sanrio spokespeople) and was blamed by the NYT for her weakness as an animated character. Pixar's Up is the documented shape-language case: circles roll and move fast, squares are stable.

Jumping spiders (reported): perceived as less frightening than other spiders because of "neotenic traits such as two larger eyes and very short legs" (Frontiers in Arachnid Science 2025, citing Bixler 2015): two large forward eyes plus short limbs override an unappealing body plan.

Does cute UI help? (measured, thin): the oft-cited "USC study, 23 % more engagement" does not exist in any findable paper. The real figure is System1/IPA Databank advertising data (mascot campaigns 37 % more likely to grow share), observational, no controls. A 2022 HCII poster (N = 166) found favourable responses to cute mobile aesthetics. Nothing tests cute agents and error forgiveness directly.

Implications for Apature: eyes at or below the head's midline, ring (iris) bright lavender with a dark centre and one shared highlight; head at least half the height of the full-body figure; no mouth in the mark; one primary shape (circle); design the error and empty-state faces first, because anthropomorphism peaks when a system is unpredictable (Epley, Waytz & Cacioppo 2007).

Sources: Gould, "Mickey Mouse Meets Konrad Lorenz" (Natural History 1979, PDF at cognitionandculture.net); Glocker 2009 (PMC3260535); Frontiers in Psychology 2022 (10.3389/fpsyg.2022.979341); Miesler, Leder & Herrmann, International Journal of Design 2011 (ijdesign.org/…/1023/363); Yao 2022 (Frontiers 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.674456); PsyPost on pupil/iris studies; Epley 2007 (PubMed 17907867); Wikipedia "Twelve basic principles of animation", "Hello Kitty"; CGW "The Shape of Animation" (2009); PBS on Dick Bruna; shmuplations Kirby 1993 interview; Monocle and nippon.com on Kumamon; Frontiers in Arachnid Science 2025 (frchs.2025.1717365); Adweek on System1/IPA.

Placement pattern (inferred from the table): in-product mascots cluster at first run, empty states, success confirmations and notifications. They are almost never in the error path or on the hot working surface. Both famous failures were interruptions of the task.

Production norms (reported): Duolingo's reshaping took months of daily iteration; silhouette, then quarter-turn, then movement; colour and species fixed up front. Ferris ships six poses and that is a complete set for a community project. Mailchimp shipped a "party pooper mode" to turn humour off after prolonged-exposure complaints.

3. Licensing model for an open-source mascot

The pattern that spreads: character art under CC BY 4.0 (Go, Android, Kiki/Wilber with SA) or CC0 (Ferris), with an explicit no-trademark clause ("you may not file trademark applications for the character or derivatives"), while the product name and app icon stay the project's marks under a short policy. Keep the artist's copyright assignment or licence separate from the trademark question, and decide the owner (the author or a foundation) before the character has value: PostgreSQL and Mastodon both ended up registering theirs.

Checklist before naming anything: USPTO Trademark Search (TESS retired Nov 2023) and EUIPO TMview in classes 9 and 42 (25 and 16 if merch); phonetic variants; Mac App Store, GitHub org and repo, crates.io, npm; domains and handles.

4. Motion and placement inside a professional tool

Standards (reported): Apple HIG Motion: "Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion … may make them feel disconnected or physically uncomfortable"; Apple's Reduce Motion criteria name "scaling, spinning, or peripheral motion" as triggers and say decorative animation should "stop entirely" under the setting. WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A): moving or blinking content that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and is presented in parallel with other content needs a pause mechanism unless essential; the intent text names attention-deficit users. WebKit's motion post names peripheral horizontal movement as a vestibular trigger; the Game Accessibility Guidelines say background animation "can distract greatly … particularly for ADHD."

Practice (reported): Duolingo blends eight head and eight body idle clips at random to avoid repetitive loops, under a megabyte per character, driven by lesson events. Kit's tail carries emotion "even in still moments." Clippy's Bayesian timing model was replaced before shipping because it "was deemed too cautious." Human blink rate is 17.6 per minute with a 4.3 s mean interval (J Neurosci 2011); animators use fast close, slower open, randomised interval. Lottie ran at ~17 fps versus Rive ~60 fps on a mid-range phone in one benchmark; rive-rs exists but is Vello-only with no production-readiness statement. For egui, a short sprite strip with timer-driven frames is the only zero-dependency path.

What photojournalists say (reported): "Granted it may not have the prettiest interface, but there is a lot of power under the hood"; Photo Mechanic "because it is wicked fast"; "14 minutes to download my photos … and upload to Team USA"; "feels a bit like working in Windows 2000." Nobody asked for personality; nobody minds it as long as it never costs a keystroke.

Rules for Apature (inferred):

  1. The mascot never animates in the contact sheet, preview, caption or metadata panes.
  2. Any animation is one-shot, finishes within 5 s and never loops.
  3. It moves only on user-caused events: job done, ingest finished, error, empty folder, first run.
  4. No unprompted appearances and no idle fidgeting while the user works.
  5. Under Reduce Motion: a static frame, or an opacity or colour change only.
  6. Blink, if used, only in the empty state and About: 100–150 ms, randomised 3–6 s, stopping after 5 s.
  7. Squash and stretch at most 5 %; small travel; never in the periphery.
  8. A "no mascot" setting ships with it.
  9. Pre-baked frames; no animation runtime until measured against decode load with the frame-time HUD.
  10. Warmth lives in copy and empty states, not in the cull loop.

Sources: developer.apple.com HIG Motion and App Store Connect Reduced Motion criteria; w3.org WCAG 2.2.2 Understanding; webkit.org motion post; alistapart.com "Designing Safer Web Animation"; gameaccessibilityguidelines.com; rive.app Duolingo Lily post; erichorvitz.com Lumière; xenon.stanford.edu Clippy thesis; PMC3156585 (blink rate); callstack.com Lottie vs Rive; github.com/rive-app/rive-rs; fstoppers and blog.jeffcable.com quotes; shutterbug.com Photo Mechanic review.

5. Names

"Lumen" (reported): Lumen Technologies, the Lumen metabolism app, the Laravel Lumen framework, Unreal Engine 5 Lumen, Lumen Database, Lumen5, an Apple Arcade game, a macOS brightness utility, and three photography apps: Lumen Camera (manual/RAW iPhone camera, 2026), Lumen Forge (exposure calculator) and Rollei Lumen (studio lights). No owl named Lumen exists in software, but in-category crowding makes it a weak choice.

"Apature" (reported): no app, npm or crate called Apature; a dormant GitHub user and an unrelated apatureai org (TypeScript UI-review tooling, June 2026); apature.com (1996, parked), .io/.net (2023, no site); .app and .dev likely free (UNVERIFIED). Apple's Aperture (discontinued 2015) and Aputure (cinema lighting) are near-homophones; search engines autocorrect to Aperture. Reported, not advice.

Less crowded character names (reported, checked by URL): Dek, Cutline, Lede are the clearest of the newsroom set; Loupe (GNOME image viewer), Sprocket (HP), Pica (font manager), Chimp (Mailchimp), Fixer, Stringer (Stringr), Byline are taken or near-taken. Unchecked: Hed, Grain, Tilt, Wire, Journal.

Animal name collisions (reported): Kingfisher = onevcat/Kingfisher, a ~24k-star Swift image-caching library in Apature's own ecosystem, plus Kingfisher plc and the beer; Firefly = Adobe Firefly (generative AI, the opposite of culling), Firefly III, Firefly Aerospace; Tarsier = Reworkd's tarsier and ByteDance's Tarsier video models; Mantis shrimp and jumping spider (salticidae npm package only) are clear fields; a homing pigeon has no software mascot found (UNVERIFIED beyond this pass).

7. Newsroom culture to draw on

Verified: "shutterbug" dates from 1938 (amateur-coded, dated among pros); "chimping" was written up by Robert Deutsch in the September 1999 SportsShooter newsletter, named for the "ooh-ooh-ooh"; "the decisive moment" is a Cardinal de Retz line surfaced by Tériade for the 1952 book; "f/8 and be there" is folklore (earliest print 1973); -30- is a live ritual sign-off; hed, dek, lede, TK, graf are deliberate misspellings so they stand out in copy; every Pulitzer photo 1942–54 was shot on a Speed Graphic; the press card in the hatband is a movie cliché. Nikon has an official mascot in Japan (Nikon-chan, plush keychain Aug 2026). The NPPA logo is a lens, no eagle. Safelights are red or amber because paper is blind to red. Lavender oil dissolved Niépce's bitumen and washed the unexposed areas of the first photographs (1826–27), and the 1832 physautotype used lavender-oil residue as the light-sensitive agent itself. The firefly (Photuris pensylvanica) is Pennsylvania's state insect by the Act of 10 April 1974.

Ideas only a newspaper photographer would clock (inferred): a pigeon with a film canister on its leg, gone when the job is "filed"; -30- on the done-culling toast and TK on the empty state; "Filed." as the export confirmation; a changing bag, a grease pencil and a lanyard press pass as the only props; an anti-chimping pose during ingest; tooltips in desk jargon, "spray-and-pray detected: 42 near-duplicates."

8. Decision options

Option A, keep the owl. Rename it (Dek, Cutline or Lede are clear; Hed and Wire unchecked), move the eyes to the lower half of the head, make the lens rings the dominant feature with one shared highlight, no mouth in the mark, charcoal body and lavender rings on a dark tile. Accept the Duolingo adjacency and never animate it on the working surface.

Option B, the homing pigeon. The story is the owner's own trade, the film canister on the leg is a prop that explains itself, "filed" states write themselves, and nobody in software owns it. Cuteness needs work: big round head, eye set low and large, stubby body, no neck. Name candidates: Wire, Journal, Souths (220 South Street, UNVERIFIED availability).

Option C, the jumping spider. The sharpest "camera eye" story in nature and a proven cute precedent, but some users will not love a spider on first launch, so it would need the mascot-off switch to be prominent. Best kept as the easter egg or the "focus" character if B wins.

Whatever wins: fix species and colour; silhouette first, tested as a black fill at 16 and 128 px; six to ten poses; CC BY 4.0 plus a no-trademark clause; the ten motion rules in §4; the empty state, About and finished-job toast as its only homes; a setting to turn it off.

9. "I really like Kit": what to take from Firefox's mascot

The owner's steer after reading the first pass. What Kit actually does, from Mozilla's announcement and the published turnaround (reported):

  • Rendered, not flat. Kit is a soft gradient body (the Firefox logo's orange to yellow to magenta) with gentle shading, on a deep purple ground; it looks drawn, not assembled from shapes. The team drew it in pencil first and derived its proportions from the logo.
  • No mouth. Eyes are large and dark with a coloured catchlight; the catchlight carries the look. Ears and the tail do the acting.
  • One signature detail. The tail "carries motion and emotion even in still moments" and wraps into a heart on the onboarding card.
  • Few, strict appearances. Getting started, discovering a feature, a small win, "then steps back so you can keep moving"; "a companion, not a commentator"; no punchlines; optional as a wallpaper.
  • A turnaround sheet of front, three-quarter and side views was published, so the character is drawable from any angle.

Applied to Apature (inferred), with sketches in mascot/kit-style/: a soft charcoal-to-lavender gradient body on the darkroom ground (our palette is already Kit's purple world); big dark eyes with a lavender catchlight instead of lens rings; no mouth; the ear tufts as the owl's signature detail (up at rest, perked for a small win, back when content) with the wings as the second; for the pigeon, the film canister and the tail fan. Appearances follow §4. Note that this is the opposite rendering language from the flat/voxel/pixel treatments in lumen-styles/; the pixel sprite stays for 16 px, everything else goes Kit's way.
